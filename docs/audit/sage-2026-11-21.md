The raw count joins HTML text nodes after excluding scripts, styles, templates, noscript and SVG, then counts English word-like segments. It includes both hero variants and the text in closed disclosures. The rendered count uses `innerText` with styles loaded and closed disclosures in their initial state. Chromium’s accessibility tree exposed one level-one heading per viewport. It contained **727 desktop** and **696 mobile** static-text words in the main landmark, with identical results before and after hydration. The project dialog and mobile menu confirmed that the JavaScript-enabled pages had hydrated. Reduced motion isolated the copy from the intro timing.

Counting only paragraph text yields 588 words in the source, or 369 desktop and 348 mobile words when applying browser visibility. These narrower measurements illustrate the effect of changing the extraction scope.

The concatenated H1 in SAGE is reproducible through `textContent`. The headline variants live in one native heading, and CSS exposes the relevant copy to the browser and accessibility tree. This explains the scanner’s combined title. A single wording across both layouts would simplify CSS-blind extraction if the hero copy is revised later. [Live homepage](https://itshamid.com/)

**The 5% result is plausible for that denominator.** The response had 92,181 decoded characters (92,328 UTF-8 bytes). Desktop and mobile rendered main text occupied 4,800 and 4,584 characters, or **5.21%** and **4.97%**. Inline script elements accounted for about 49,670 characters, including Next.js data, JSON-LD and bootstrap code. The stable main counts with JavaScript disabled establish that the copy is present in the initial HTML. Google sets no preferred word-count target. ## Scanner false positives and feature suggestions

- The thin-content and JavaScript-rendering implications do not follow from the observed homepage. The three scanner modules use different checks, and the crawlable page already includes work, services, reviews and disclosures.
- The description-length warning is a display heuristic. Google can choose or truncate a result snippet based on the query and device. Homepage breadcrumb, language-alternate and publication-date prompts are inapplicable to the current English root page. Articles already expose their publication dates and bylines. [Google snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)
- Fixed answer lengths, definition patterns, tables and heading IDs are optional editorial choices. Useful section destinations already have anchors on the Growth Engine and article pages. The homepage is primarily an introduction and a route to the work, services and contact options. [Google guidance for generative search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- The CSV’s suggested `Speakable`, `FAQPage` and WebMCP features do not solve any verified defect here. Google’s Speakable documentation concerns topical news voice playback, and its FAQ rich-result guidance targets government and health sites. Chrome documents WebMCP as early-preview browser interaction. Ordinary HTML disclosures and links already serve this page. [Speakable](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/speakable) · [FAQ guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes) · [Chrome WebMCP](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/webmcp/webmcp)
- The script count alone cannot establish a loading regression. The inline checks support the first-load intro and analytics consent, and the audit did not measure an attributable timing problem.
